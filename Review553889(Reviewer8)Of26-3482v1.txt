The paper proposes ADAPT, a perceptive locomotion framework for
humanoid robots. ADAPT decomposes local 3D geometry into two compact
projections: a horizontal elevation map for footholds and a vertical
distance map for body-level clearance. Moreover, it adds the sensing
radius as an extra action of the reinforcement learning policy. The
authors compare ADAPT  in simulation against PIM, Humanoid Parkour, a
TSDF, and Gallant, and deployed zero-shot on a Unitree G1.

To my knowledge, the content of the article is novel and sufficiently
significant. The authors justify their method well and are clear in the
explanation of the limits of baseline methods and the strengths of
their proposed technique. The experiments validate the authors
assumptions. 
The organization of the paper is adequate, with one exception listed
below. to my knowledge, the citations are adequate.

I believe this to be a valuable contribution, still I believe some
parts could be clearer.

1. There is a possible typo in the introduction: “3-D negotiation
tasks”, you mean “3-D navigation tasks”?

2. In the introduction, you claim that the adaptive sensing mechanism
is responsible for reducing the computational overhead by 56%. Where in
the experiment section do you support this claim? The same question
holds for the 98.6% reduction in observation dimensionality with
respect to voxel-based methods that is claimed in the abstract. I think
this comparison could be more clearly stated.

3. The abstract, introduction, and Sec. II-B say the radius modulates
the vertical map only. Sec. III-B-1 says it acts as "a dynamic gain
modulating the field of view for both projection maps." Is the
horizontal map also influenced by the radius? 

4. Was the ablation study on the adaptive radius mechanism done in
simulation or in the real-world? Discussing Table V results in Sec.
IV-C-1 creates confusion.

5. The ablation shows that without the adaptive reward the policy fails
to learn the optimal radius as speed increases. Do you have an
explanation on why that happens? Which part of the adaptive reward is
responsible for retrieving the optimal policy? Is the optimal reward
basically enforcing a hand-designed heuristic? Understanding this could
help better define the scope of the contribution.

6. Fig.1 makes it look like the actor also receives the privileged
states, which contradicts what is claimed by the text in Sec. III-C. I
also think Fig.1 could be more informative if it showed the recurrent
nature of the actor. 

7. Fig. 5 only shows the average success rates, it would be better to
show also confidence intervals/variance. The plot as it is does not
give information on how much performance varies across seeds.
