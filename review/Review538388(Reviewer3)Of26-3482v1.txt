This paper presents ADAPT, a perceptive locomotion framework for
humanoid robots that decomposes local 3D geometry into two projections:
a horizontal elevation map and a vertical distance map. The proposed
approach is evaluated against several representative baselines and is
further deployed on a Unitree G1 humanoid robot in the real world. My
comments are as follows.

1. This paper addresses an important problem in perceptive locomotion,
and the motivation is well established.

2. Incorporating the perception radius into the policy’s action space
is a clever design choice. It allows the robot to adaptively adjust its
perceptual range according to the requirements of different 3D
locomotion tasks.

3. Table III shows that ADAPT outperforms all baselines, including some
computationally intensive voxel-based approaches. Given that voxel
representations retain richer 3D geometric information, it would be
helpful for the authors to provide further analysis explaining why
these methods perform worse than ADAPT. In particular, the paper should
clarify whether the performance gap arises from optimization
difficulty, limited network capacity, training-budget constraints,
representation redundancy, or other implementation factors.

4. The real-world experiments provide valuable hardware validation.
However, for the drop experiment, it is difficult to determine whether
the robot recovers its balance autonomously or whether its fall is
arrested by the safety tether.

5. Table IV reports training efficiency, but inference efficiency is
arguably more important for perceptive locomotion because training is
performed offline, whereas perception and control must operate under
strict real-time constraints during deployment. The authors are
encouraged to report inference-time benchmarks, including
map-construction latency, policy inference latency, total
perception-to-control latency, and onboard computational resource
usage. A comparison of these quantities across ADAPT and the main
baselines would better support the paper’s efficiency claims.
