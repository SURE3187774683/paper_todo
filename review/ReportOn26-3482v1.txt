This paper received two higly qualified reviews and both reviwers agree
that the paper addresses a relavant problem in perceptive locomotion,
is well motivated and proposes an interesting approach to cope with
different requirements of 3D real-world locomotion tasks, depending on
how far ahead it perceives obstacles. The environment is compacted into
two perspectives: vertical and horizontal and the policy additionally
outputs the sensing radius as an action. ADAPT is compared in
simulation against PIM, Humanoid Parkour, a TSDF-based method, and
Gallant, and is deployed zero-shot on a Unitree G1.


Both reviewers highlight unsupported claims in the paper:

-	Authors claim that ADAPT provides a more compact representation
and potentially makes reinforcement learning more computationally
efficient, compared with voxel-based alternatives. The authors should
specify how the comparisons were conducted. They have to provide enough
detail to verify both the dimensionality reduction and the
training-speed comparison. In particular, R8 asks where the 56%
computational overhead reduction and the 98.6%
observation-dimensionality reduction are supported. R3 notes that Table
IV reports only training efficiency, whereas inference efficiency
matters more for real-time perceptive control. R3 also asks for latency
and onboard resource measurements against the main baselines. 

The results need deeper analysis:
-	Note that voxel-based representations are not inherently
inferior; they simply encode the environment differently and may have
different computational trade-offs. R3 mentions that authors should
analyze why the voxel-based baselines underperform, addressing the
possible factors: optimization difficulty, network capacity, training
budget, representation redundancy, or implementation factors
-	Explain the role of the adaptive reward in learning the optimal
radius, and clarify how much of it encodes a hand-designed heuristic.

Clarifications and presentation.
-	(R8) Resolve the inconsistency over whether the radius
modulates only the vertical map (abstract, introduction, Sec. II-B) or
both maps (Sec. III-B-1).
-	(R8) State whether the adaptive-radius ablation (Table V, Sec.
IV-C-1) was run in simulation or on hardware, and fix the discussion in
that section accordingly.
-	(R3) For the drop experiment, make clear whether the robot
recovers balance autonomously or the safety tether arrests the fall.
-	(R8)Add confidence intervals or variance across seeds to Fig.
5.
-	(R8) Fix the likely typo “3-D negotiation tasks” in the
introduction (probably “navigation”).
